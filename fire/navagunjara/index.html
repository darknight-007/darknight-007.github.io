Fire · Navagunjara Reborn

A god, assembled from nine animals and two continents

In Odisha's retelling of the Mahabharata, Arjuna meets a creature made of nine animals — rooster, peacock, bull, lion, elephant, tiger, deer, serpent, and a human arm holding a lotus — and must decide whether to shoot or to bow. He lowers his bow. The Navagunjara is the test of whether you can recognize the divine in a form you were never taught to expect.

Navagunjara Reborn fused that figure with the Phoenix and built it at eighteen feet: dhokra lost-wax bronze, bamboo cane, sabai grass, pattachitra painting, Pipili appliqué, and Kotpad textiles from master artisans across Odisha, carried on an aluminum superstructure engineered in Arizona, with flame effects breathing at the head. It was designed in Blender, guided to artisans through 1:1 orthographic templates generated by DeepGIS, scanned back by photogrammetry, and assembled for the first time — never rehearsed, not even partially — on the playa, through three storms.

After the 2025 burn, parts of the sculpture were stolen; others weathered; the surviving crafts stayed under a U.S. temporary importation bond. In 2026 the god returned with a dragon's head of wired aluminum scales, carrying its surviving cane and bronze into a second incarnation. Some saw a dragon, some a tiger, a lion, a gazelle, a dinosaur. The truth, as the myth insists, is multifaceted.

The record, run backward

The film of the 2026 build runs in reverse — from the finished god to the hands that made it. So does this timeline.

  1. September 2026 · Black Rock City

    The god stands, breathes fire, and is released

    Flame at the head like a carried moon. E-bikes circling at two in the morning. The caption afterward names everyone and mentions no loss.

  2. August 2026 · the playa, in a whiteout

    Build week

    Propane plumbing in a dust storm, a scissor lift, the neighboring art ghosted in the haze. The surviving tigress leg and cane go back up exactly where the customs declaration said they would.

  3. Summer 2026 · Reno

    A dragon's head, scale by scale

    Cut aluminum scales wired into a lattice — the dragon's skin as literal chainmail — and sheet-metal head plates clamped and fitted. The lóng, in Chinese tradition, is itself a composite of nine animals: a nine-in-one head for a nine-in-one god.

  4. Spring 2026 · the workshop

    The frame goes up again

    Surviving crafts are reintegrated, lost components re-specified rather than replicated, the aluminum spine rebuilt around what remains. Reincarnation, not restoration: the pattern persists, the body changes.

  5. September 2025 – July 2026 · the interregnum

    Theft, storms, and a federal bond

    A ninety-minute theft during teardown. A sheriff's report. Flame and weather damage disclosed honestly on every manifest. A customs extension requested with full documentation and granted in a day. The year the god spent as paperwork — all of it kept.

  6. August 2025 · Black Rock City

    First incarnation

    One-shot full assembly on playa, 700 feet from The Man, through three storms, with hand tools when the dust made power tools impossible. It stood. Wind through the hollow aluminum made it sing — a sound no one composed.

  7. July 2025 · Phoenix

    Crates arrive

    Air freight from Odisha, each crate labeled with an orthographic print of its own contents. Six weeks to design the superstructure around the artisans' forms rather than forcing the forms to fit the structure.

  8. April – May 2025 · Odisha

    Craft

    Pipili appliqué at a treadle machine. Dhokra bronze cast by lost wax and checked against a 3D print on a bedspread. Sabai grass woven over steel wire. Cane bent into rings by hand, with a curvature no CAD tool was asked to specify.

  9. The beginning · Kalasastra Foundation, Odisha

    Hands

    Trace any made thing far enough upstream and you arrive here: a floor, patience, and a plant that grows back whether or not anyone builds a god from it.
